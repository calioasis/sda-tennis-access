Community recreation during non-school hours

SDA Tennis Access

Exploring a practical joint-use model for community access to San Dieguito Academy tennis courts.

San Dieguito Academy has tennis courts that appear well-positioned for a controlled community-access model during non-school hours. Nearby cities and school districts already use joint-use agreements to make school recreational facilities, including high school tennis courts, available to the public while preserving school use and addressing maintenance, liability, scheduling, and security.

View nearby models Download agreements

The idea

The idea is simple: evaluate whether the SDA tennis courts could be made available for public recreation during non-school hours through a formal agreement between San Dieguito Union High School District and the City of Encinitas.

This would not mean opening the entire campus. It would mean studying whether this specific court area could function as a controlled joint-use pilot site, with school use preserved and public access limited to appropriate times.

Why SDA may be a strong candidate

SDA appears to be a particularly strong candidate because the tennis courts are located near the edge of campus and are directly accessible from the street. Public use would not require people to walk through the school campus, classroom areas, or interior student spaces.

That site layout may make SDA different from schools where courts or fields are located deep inside campus.

Nearby joint-use models

This is not a request for SDUHSD to invent a new model. Nearby cities and school districts already use joint-use agreements to make school recreational facilities, including high school tennis courts, available to the public while preserving school use and addressing maintenance, liability, scheduling, and security.

Mission Bay High School / City of San Diego

The City of San Diego and San Diego Unified School District have a joint-use agreement for Mission Bay High School tennis courts. The agreement preserves school use during school hours and provides city/community recreation use after school, on weekends, holidays, and other non-school days.

The agreement also addresses practical issues such as maintenance, operations, insurance, liability, and gate/opening responsibilities.

Carlsbad Unified / City of Carlsbad

Carlsbad Unified School District and the City of Carlsbad have had a joint-use/community recreation arrangement since 1989. The current agreement includes Carlsbad High School tennis courts.

Covered facilities are available for public use during city park operating hours, generally 8 a.m. to 10 p.m., except when school is in session or the district has reserved the facility. In exchange for public/community use, the City of Carlsbad performs routine maintenance through its Parks & Recreation operations.

What joint-use agreements can solve

The important takeaway is not simply that other districts allow access. It is that formal joint-use agreements can define who is responsible for the difficult operational details.

  • School priority and blackout periods
  • Public access hours
  • Scheduling and reservations
  • Routine maintenance
  • Cleanup
  • Gate opening and closing
  • Insurance
  • Liability and indemnity
  • Cost sharing or city maintenance responsibilities
  • Coordination between district and city staff

What still needs to be studied

This proposal still requires real evaluation. Important questions include:

  • Would SDUHSD be open to studying SDA as a pilot site?
  • Would the City of Encinitas be willing to participate?
  • What maintenance, resurfacing, and cleanup costs would need to be covered?
  • Who would open, close, and secure the gates?
  • What hours would be appropriate?
  • How would school use, team use, tournaments, and reservations take priority?
  • Would additional signage, fencing, locks, or scheduling systems be needed?

Current status

This page is intended as a resource for community members, SDUHSD trustees, district staff, and City of Encinitas staff who may be interested in evaluating whether a joint-use model could work at SDA.

Existing joint-use agreements from nearby jurisdictions are being gathered and summarized so the conversation can be based on proven models rather than speculation.

The goal is not to demand an immediate answer. The goal is to ask whether SDA can be evaluated as a practical pilot site.
